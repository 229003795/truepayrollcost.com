Patriot software pricing: count three different billing quantities

The illustrated Full Service Payroll schedule below costs $891 at regular rates: $37 × 12 + $5 × 45 + $6 × 12 + $2 × 27 + $12 × 8 = $891. It has forty-five paid-worker months, twenty-seven time-card-user months and eight additional-state months. Treating every quantity as five workers and three filing states all year would give $1,224, a $333 difference. These are our editable examples, not a customer invoice, promotional quote or complete employer budget.

Start with the published payroll rate

The Patriot official payroll pricing and billing FAQ · checked November 1, 2026 lists Basic at $17 monthly plus $4 per worker paid, and Full Service at $37 plus $5 per worker paid. The same page says the payroll base remains due in a month with no payroll, and lists the optional Time & Attendance and HR products at $6 monthly plus $2 per employee, each.

Basic leaves tax filing and deposits to you; Full Service adds that service. The $300 difference is not your internal filing cost, tax liability or guaranteed saving. Wages, employer taxes, benefit funding and any separately quoted services are outside these software subtotals.

A paid worker is not necessarily a time-card user

For payroll, count each distinct worker paid during each billing month, not the number of pay runs. For the time product, the Patriot official Time & Attendance usage FAQ · checked November 1, 2026 says the employee charge follows time-card usage in the billing period. Do not automatically copy payroll headcount into that field. A person paid a salary may not use a time card; a time-card record and its payroll payment may also fall in different periods.

For filing states, the Patriot official multi-state filing rate and scope · checked November 1, 2026 includes the first filing state with Full Service and prices each additional state at $12 monthly. Count additional billed filing states, not all work locations. Confirm when an added state starts and stops being billed; an employee leaving a state does not establish the end of filing obligations or fees.

Reproduce the $891 schedule

Assume the Full Service subscription and Time & Attendance remain enabled for twelve regular-price months. In the first nine months, five distinct workers are paid monthly and three employees use time cards; the last three months have no paid workers and no time-card users. Separately assume a written billing schedule confirms two additional filing states are charged for only the first four months. This last condition is deliberately supplied, not inferred from payroll activity.

The aggregate proof is paid-worker months = 5 × 9 + 0 × 3 = 45; time-card-user months = 3 × 9 + 0 × 3 = 27; additional-state months = 2 × 4 = 8. The two base subscriptions contribute $37 × 12 = $444 and $6 × 12 = $72. Variable charges contribute $5 × 45 = $225, $2 × 27 = $54 and $12 × 8 = $96. Therefore $444 + $72 + $225 + $54 + $96 = $891.

Contrast a flat-count assumption: ($37 + $5 × 5 + $6 + $2 × 5 + $12 × 2) × 12 = $102 × 12 = $1,224. Its excess is $75 for fifteen extra paid-worker months, $66 for thirty-three extra time-card-user months, and $192 for sixteen extra-state months: $75 + $66 + $192 = $333. This is a reconciliation of different inputs, not a claim that a provider billed incorrectly.

If the additional states are actually billable throughout the year, use twenty-four additional-state months instead of eight. That adds $12 × (24 − 8) = $192 and raises this example to $1,083. If the state schedule is unknown, the complete subtotal is pending rather than $891.

Keep promotions and other charges separate

The pricing page advertises a thirty-day trial followed by fifty percent off for three months. For five paid workers, the displayed discounted Full Service rates give $18.50 + $2.50 × 5 = $31 monthly. If those terms apply to each of three paid billing months, twelve paid months with constant headcount give $31 × 3 + $62 × 9 = $651. This is not the first 365 days after signup: trial days and billing dates must be reconciled separately. Eligibility, add-on discounts and state-fee treatment need confirmation. The $891 worked account and tool use regular prices only.

Do not append a standard setup or year-end filing fee to Full Service when the relevant scope is included. Basic customers considering electronic contractor filing should instead check the Patriot official 1099 e-filing scope and fee schedule · checked November 1, 2026; that page distinguishes Basic and Accounting from Full Service. Amendments, late work, state registration, accounting and benefit-provider charges still need their own scope and evidence. We have not verified a historical price series or obtained a customer quote.

Items to confirm before signing

  • Exact plan and whether tax filing, standard setup and year-end work are included.
  • Distinct workers actually billed in every period, including contractor payments.
  • Time-product subscription months and billable time-card users, recorded separately.
  • Additional filing states, written billing start/end dates and continuing filing obligations.
  • Offer eligibility and which charges are discounted; map trial days to actual invoice dates.
  • Any exceptional work, third-party charges, applicable sales tax and your internal transition cost.

Questions about Patriot payroll pricing

How much is Patriot Full Service for five paid workers?

At regular rates it is $62 monthly, or $744 for twelve identical months before selected extras. The calculation is ($37 + $5 × 5) × 12. The $891 example adds an explicitly different time-card and state-billing schedule; it is not a universal five-person price.

Does a month with no payroll make the whole bill zero?

No: the payroll base remains due. In our regular-price example, zero paid workers still leaves the $37 payroll base and the $6 time-product base because that optional subscription is assumed to stay enabled. Confirm separately whether state filing fees or other products remain billable.

Should time-card users always equal paid workers?

No: reconcile the two counts independently. Payroll uses distinct workers paid in the month; the official time-product FAQ uses employees with time-card usage in the billing period. Count each unit once in the appropriate period, then retain the underlying invoices and usage records.
