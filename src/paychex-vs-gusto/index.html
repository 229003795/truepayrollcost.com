Paychex vs Gusto: compare the annual bill, not a starting price

Gusto Simple for five people in one state is $948 for twelve months: ($49 + $6 × 5) × 12. The same first-year subtotal can hide different renewal arithmetic

All quote-side amounts in this section are authored assumptions, not Paychex charges, a customer invoice or a negotiated offer. Assume the written structure states $20 per month, $24 per billed run, four first-year run waivers, $60 setup, and $120 recurring annual charges. Use 26 runs in each year. The renewal bridge is $24 × 4 − $60 = $36. The run waiver ends, adding $96; setup disappears, removing $60. That is why neither the first-year difference nor the disappearance of setup alone answers the following-year question. No historical price increase is asserted.

If the following-year calendar has 27 billed runs rather than 26, this hypothetical structure becomes $240 + $24 × 27 + $120 = $1,008, or $60 above the unchanged $948 subscription. That extra run adds $24, not a monthly fee. Use your actual pay dates, including off-cycle billing rules, rather than assuming the payroll frequency label fixes the invoice count.

Find the run-price ceiling before accepting the waiver

For the five-person Gusto subscription benchmark, the first-year break-even run charge is ($948 − $240 − $60 − $120) ÷ (26 − 4) = $24. The following-year ceiling is ($948 − $240 − $120) ÷ 26 = $22.615384…. If the fee is charged to the cent, $22.61 produces $240 + 26 × $22.61 + $120 = $947.86; $22.62 produces $948.12. Rounding the ceiling upward changes which side of the benchmark the subtotal falls on.

If every run is included or all scheduled runs are waived, there is no finite per-run ceiling to calculate from billed runs. Compare the non-run charges directly. An unknown annual fee also prevents a complete ceiling calculation; do not fill it with zero merely to obtain an answer.

Check the service scope before treating either subtotal as comparable

Gusto lists Simple for one-state payroll, and Plus for multi-state payroll. Its published Plus rates are $80 per month plus $12 per person, so five people give ($80 + $12 × 5) × 12 = $1,680. That is a different scope benchmark from $948; do not compare a multi-state proposal with Simple merely because Simple has the lower sticker price. Keep switching work outside the supplier cash line

Supplier cash subtotal = recurring service charges + confirmed one-time supplier charges. Internal transition cost = your documented transition hours × your internal hourly cost. Combined cost = the cash subtotal + internal transition cost only after both amounts are known. A blank estimate does not mean switching takes no work.

Do not translate a vendor's survey-average time saving into your company's cost. Nor does a cheaper software subtotal establish lower total employer payroll expense: wages, employer taxes, benefits and insurance remain outside this page. Carry your own quote into the homepage annual-cost calculator when you have the separate internal-work inputs.

Items to confirm before signing

  1. Product and headcount: same people, states and service requirements; extend per-person charges before comparing totals.
  2. Processing unit: unlimited included, per-run, per-check or another basis; include corrections and off-cycle runs only if the agreement bills them.
  3. Waiver: exactly which fees, how many events and which dates; no assumed renewal.
  4. Setup: amount and whether it truly disappears after implementation.
  5. Recurring extras: year-end forms, tax services, state registrations, support and other modules; mark included instead of charging twice.
  6. Renewal: stated rates, notice periods and effective dates; the calculator assumes no rate changes.
  7. Switching work: hours and rate documented separately, plus unresolved supplier charges marked pending.

Questions answered directly

Is Paychex cheaper than Gusto for five people?

You cannot establish that from an unpriced Paychex package page. Gusto Simple's unchanged five-person subscription example is $948 per complete year. Extend the written Paychex quote on the same service scope and include separately confirmed charges before comparing it.

Does a waived processing fee mean processing is always extra?

No; only your written billing terms establish a separate processing charge. The public Paychex package page lists unlimited processing. A waiver in an authored example is not evidence of a Paychex charge or promotion.

Can a first-year tie cost more at renewal without a rate increase?

Yes; changing fee applicability can change the annual subtotal even when each rate stays fixed. In this illustration a $96 waiver expires and $60 setup disappears, producing a $36 net increase. Check renewal dates and billed event counts rather than assuming either item repeats.
