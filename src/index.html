Start with the cost model

A base price is a starting point.

True Payroll Cost helps small businesses examine payroll software quotes. This first guide gives you a comparison method and a working budget worksheet—not a provider ranking. Keep wages, employer taxes, and benefit funding separate from software and administration costs.

01 / RECURRING

Count what is billed.

Record the base charge, the per-person charge, and the definition of a billable person. Ask whether inactive staff, contractors, extra entities, or additional states change your quote. These are questions to confirm, not fees we assume every provider charges.

02 / SCOPE

Price the same job.

List the services you need before comparing plans: payroll processing, tax filing, time tracking, benefits administration, and support. Mark each as included, optional, or not confirmed. An unconfirmed item is not a zero-cost item.

03 / TERMS

Read beyond month one.

Write down the normal price, promotion end date, cancellation terms, and any quoted setup charge. Separate recurring obligations from a temporary offer so that a discount does not stand in for a full-year comparison.

Make the assumptions visible

Your quote, worked through.

Enter amounts from a written quote or your own internal assumptions. All amounts use USD. Enter zero only when you have confirmed no charge applies. Blank fields are deliberately not treated as free.

One-time costs can include quoted setup fees and any overlapping service you choose to include. Avoid double-counting an item.

The formula
Monthly = base + (per-person fee × billable people) + add-ons.
First year = (monthly × 12 months) + one-time external costs + (transition hours × hourly cost).
Source of every input: your entries. The 12-month multiplier defines this worksheet's annual scenario.

Before you move providers

Ask about the exit,
not just the entrance.

Switching is a project as well as a subscription decision. Use this checklist to request a migration plan and identify which tasks belong to you, the outgoing provider, and the new provider.

“Please itemize the normal recurring price, selected extras, transition charges, cancellation terms, and who handles each migration task.”

  • Records: Which payroll reports and employee records can you export, in what format, and how will access work after cancellation?
  • Responsibility: Who handles year-to-date imports and any outstanding filings or corrections? Get the handoff documented.
  • Timing: Which pay date is the cutover, what funding deadlines apply, and will you pay for overlapping subscriptions?
  • People and systems: Who checks employee setup, deduction balances, accounting connections, and the first payroll results?
  • Charges: Are setup, data conversion, cancellation, or post-cancellation support included in your written quote?
