What this calculation does—and does not do

The formula is per-paycheck deductions × pay periods. It assumes the entered deduction is representative of each period. It does not reproduce IRS withholding tables, adjust for wage caps, or predict a final tax return. Bonuses, overtime, benefit changes and Social Security caps can make actual periods differ.

Keep employee and employer sides separate

Federal withholding, state withholding, insurance, retirement and garnishments are employee-side lines. Employer Social Security, Medicare, FUTA and state employer taxes are separate costs; use the employer payroll tax calculator for that side.

Confirm before annual budgeting

  • Use the actual pay frequency from the payroll calendar: 12 monthly, 24 semi-monthly, 26 biweekly or 52 weekly.
  • Check whether deductions change during the year or stop at an annual limit.
  • Compare the annualized estimate with year-to-date payroll records.
  • Confirm federal withholding using IRS Publication 15-T; this page only multiplies amounts you enter.

Questions people ask

Should I use 24 or 26 periods?

Use the number on the employer's payroll calendar; semi-monthly is usually 24 and biweekly is usually 26.

Does annualizing make the tax amount exact?

No. It is a planning estimate when the per-paycheck deduction stays representative.

Does this include employer payroll taxes?

No. It annualizes employee-side deductions only.
