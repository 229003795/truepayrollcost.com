The formula and the W-4 boundary

Annual estimate = (regular federal withholding per paycheck + extra W-4 Step 4(c) amount) × pay periods. The regular amount should come from the payroll register or withholding calculation; this page does not reproduce the IRS percentage method or decide W-4 Step 2, 3 or 4(a)/(b) entries.

26 or 27 paychecks in 2026? Check the anchor, not the year

The annual estimate above multiplies one paycheck by 26. That count is a calendar fact, not a constant: a biweekly schedule can pay 27 times in a year. Which one you get is decided by the first payday, not by the year number. If your first paycheck lands on January 1, the 14-day cycle fits 27 paydays into the year (the last falls on December 31). Start on January 2 and you get 26 (the last falls on December 18). One extra paycheck at $181 per check moves the annual estimate from $4,706.00 to $4,887.00.

Check your own pay-period count

Measured with Node.js v22.22.2, counting every 14th day from the first payday up to but not including January 1 of the next year. The same two anchor dates give the same result in 2025, 2026 and 2027: a January 1 anchor yields 27 paydays, a January 2 anchor yields 26. The weekday changes by year; the count does not. A 27-paycheck year is sometimes called a "third paycheck month" or a "27th pay period" — the extra check is not a raise, and it does not change your W-4. It is simply one more withholding event in that calendar year.

Do not confuse withholding with payroll tax
Federal income-tax withholding is an employee-side paycheck deduction. Employer Social Security, Medicare, FUTA and state employer taxes are separate costs. Use the employer payroll tax calculator for the employer side and the payroll deduction calculator for the complete employee-side deduction list.
Confirm before using the annual estimate
  • Use the actual pay frequency and whether the worker has multiple jobs or a working spouse.
  • Check W-4 Step 3 credits and Step 4(a)/(b) adjustments with the payroll system.
  • Recheck the estimate after bonuses, overtime, benefit changes or a new W-4.
  • Compare the result with the employer's payroll register and current IRS Publication 15-T.

Questions people ask
Does extra W-4 withholding change employer payroll tax?
No. It changes the employee's federal income-tax withholding only.
Should I use 24 or 26 pay periods?
Use the pay frequency on the payroll calendar; semi-monthly is usually 24 and biweekly is usually 26.
Why might I get 27 paychecks in a year?
Biweekly means every 14 days, and 365 days does not divide evenly by 14. A year has 26 full biweekly cycles plus a remainder, so when your first payday falls early enough in January (for example January 1), the 27th payday still lands inside the same calendar year. Check the anchor date in the tool above rather than assuming 26.
Does this calculate the correct W-4 amount?
No. It annualizes amounts you enter; the correct W-4 inputs depend on the employee's facts and IRS tables.
