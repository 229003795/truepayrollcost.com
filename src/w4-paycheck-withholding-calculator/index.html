The formula and the W-4 boundary

Annual estimate = (regular federal withholding per paycheck + extra W-4 Step 4(c) amount) × pay periods. The regular amount should come from the payroll register or withholding calculation; this page does not reproduce the IRS percentage method or decide W-4 Step 2, 3 or 4(a)/(b) entries.

Do not confuse withholding with payroll tax

Federal income-tax withholding is an employee-side paycheck deduction. Employer Social Security, Medicare, FUTA and state employer taxes are separate costs. Use the employer payroll tax calculator for the employer side and the payroll deduction calculator for the complete employee-side deduction list.

Confirm before using the annual estimate

  • Use the actual pay frequency and whether the worker has multiple jobs or a working spouse.
  • Check W-4 Step 3 credits and Step 4(a)/(b) adjustments with the payroll system.
  • Recheck the estimate after bonuses, overtime, benefit changes or a new W-4.
  • Compare the result with the employer's payroll register and current IRS Publication 15-T.

Questions people ask

Does extra W-4 withholding change employer payroll tax?

No. It changes the employee's federal income-tax withholding only.

Should I use 24 or 26 pay periods?

Use the pay frequency on the payroll calendar; semi-monthly is usually 24 and biweekly is usually 26.

Does this calculate the correct W-4 amount?

No. It annualizes amounts you enter; the correct W-4 inputs depend on the employee's facts and IRS tables.
