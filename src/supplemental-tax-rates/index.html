Supplemental tax rates 2026: 22% and the $1 million rule

For separately identified supplemental wages, the 2026 federal flat withholding method is 22%; the excess over $1,000,000 of an employee’s calendar-year supplemental wages is withheld at 37%. This is federal income-tax withholding, not the employee’s final tax bill or a state/local rate.

What the rates do—and do not—answer

QuestionAnswer
What is 22%?An optional flat federal income-tax withholding rate for separately identified supplemental wages.
When does 37% apply?To the excess when the employee’s calendar-year supplemental wages exceed $1 million.
Are state and local rates included?No. They vary by jurisdiction and payroll setup; confirm them separately.
Is withholding the final tax?No. It is an amount withheld toward the employee’s tax liability.

Bonuses are not the only supplemental wages

Supplemental wages can include bonuses, commissions, overtime, vacation pay and certain other payments. If supplemental wages are combined with regular wages and not separately identified, the employer may need a different withholding method. Follow the employee’s Form W-4, payroll records and the applicable IRS instructions.

Confirmation checklist

  • Identify the payment as supplemental wages and document whether it is separately stated.
  • Track the employee’s calendar-year supplemental total before applying the $1 million threshold.
  • Confirm federal method, state/local rules, Social Security, Medicare and Additional Medicare Tax separately.
  • Keep the written payroll calculation and the applicable IRS publication with the payroll record.

FAQ

What is the 2026 supplemental wage tax rate?

The federal optional flat withholding rate is 22% for separately identified supplemental wages; the excess over $1 million in the calendar year is 37%.

Does 22% mean the employee owes 22% tax?

No. It is a federal withholding method, not a final tax calculation.

Can this page calculate every bonus deduction?

No. State/local withholding and the employee’s full federal facts require the employer’s payroll process and current IRS instructions.
