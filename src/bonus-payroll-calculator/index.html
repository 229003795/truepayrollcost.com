Federal supplemental withholding is not the same as employer cost

For separately identified supplemental wages, IRS Publication 15 describes an optional 22% federal flat withholding method; the excess over $1 million of calendar-year supplemental wages is subject to 37%. That withholding is not the employee’s final tax and does not replace the employer-side tax lines above.

What to confirm before paying a bonus

  • Whether the payment is separately identified as supplemental wages.
  • Employee Social Security wage-base status and any Additional Medicare Tax handling.
  • FUTA, state unemployment, local taxes, workers’ compensation and benefit effects.
  • Whether the written payroll register shows gross bonus, employee withholding and employer cost as separate lines.

FAQ

Does employee withholding reduce employer bonus cost?

No. It reduces the employee’s payment; employer cost starts with gross bonus pay and adds applicable employer-side taxes and benefits.

Is $1,076.50 a guaranteed cost for a $1,000 bonus?

No. It uses the stated Social Security and Medicare fixture only. FUTA, state and other items may change the result.

Does this calculate the employee’s final tax?

No. It separates an employer-cost fixture from federal supplemental withholding and final tax liability.
