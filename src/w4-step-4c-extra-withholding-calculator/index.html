W-4 Step 4(c) extra withholding calculator — one job or split across two

Put it on one job: a $1,200 annual shortfall ÷ 20 remaining paydays = $60 per check on Step 4(c). Split the same $1,200 across two jobs: $600 ÷ 20 = $30 and $600 ÷ 12 = $50. Same annual total, different per-check amounts because each job has its own payday count.

Both are the same division of an amount you already estimated. Step 4(c) is where the extra amount is written on the W-4; having more than one job changes whether you write one number or two. This page does both, so pick the mode that matches your situation — you do not need two pages.

How the split works

The annual shortfall is divided between jobs by the percentage you choose, then each job's share is divided by that job's remaining paydays. The IRS says you may apply the extra amount to one job or divide it between jobs; each job receiving a change needs a new Form W-4.

What this calculator does not decide

It does not determine your annual tax liability, run the IRS percentage method, or decide whether your estimate is correct. Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator or Publication 505 for the underlying shortfall and check the result with payroll. Do not confuse this employee-side adjustment with employer payroll tax.

For the broader employee deduction list, use the payroll deduction calculator; for known withholding annualization, use the W-4 paycheck withholding calculator.

Check before filing a new W-4

  • Use the number of paydays remaining, not a generic 12-month count.
  • If more than one job is involved, decide which job receives the extra amount or divide it deliberately.
  • Recheck after a bonus, job change, spouse-job change or revised estimate.
  • Review IRS Publication 505 and Publication 15-T, checked November 26, 2026.

Questions people ask

Does this tell me what my total W-4 should be?

No. It estimates only the additional Step 4(c) amount from inputs you provide.

Can I split the extra amount between two jobs?

Yes. The IRS explains that you can apply the amount to one job or divide it between jobs — use Mode 2 above.

Can I put all extra withholding on one job?

Yes. The IRS allows applying it to one job or dividing it between jobs — use Mode 1 above.

Why are the two per-check amounts different in Mode 2?

Each amount uses that job's own remaining paydays, so the same dollar share becomes a different per-check figure.

Does this replace the Multiple Jobs Worksheet?

No. It only allocates an annual amount you already estimated.

What if I have no remaining paydays?

Do not calculate a zero-payday result; ask payroll or use the IRS estimator for the next filing step.
