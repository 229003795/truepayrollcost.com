What this number means

This is a transparent division: an amount you want withheld over the remaining paydays divided by those paydays. The IRS says you can apply the amount to one job or divide it between jobs; use the actual paydays remaining for the job receiving the new Form W-4.

What this calculator does not decide

It does not determine your annual tax liability, run the IRS percentage method, or decide whether your estimate is correct. Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator or Publication 505 for the underlying shortfall and check the result with payroll.

Do not confuse this employee-side adjustment with employer payroll tax. For the broader employee deduction list, use the payroll deduction calculator; for known withholding annualization, use the W-4 paycheck withholding calculator.

Check before filing a new W-4

  • Use the number of paydays remaining, not a generic 12-month count.
  • If more than one job is involved, decide which job receives the extra amount or divide it deliberately.
  • Recheck after a bonus, job change, spouse-job change or revised estimate.
  • Review IRS Publication 505 and Publication 15-T, checked November 15, 2026.

Questions people ask

Does this tell me what my total W-4 should be?

No. It estimates only the additional Step 4(c) amount from inputs you provide.

Can I split the extra amount between two jobs?

Yes. The IRS explains that you can apply the amount to one job or divide it between jobs.

What if I have no remaining paydays?

Do not calculate a zero-payday result; ask payroll or use the IRS estimator for the next filing step.
