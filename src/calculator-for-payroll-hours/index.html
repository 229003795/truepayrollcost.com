Minutes are not hundredths of an hour

Decimal hours = whole hours + remaining minutes ÷ 60. Seven hours thirty minutes is 7 + 30 ÷ 60 = 7.50 hours, not 7.30. Entering 7.30 into a decimal-hour field loses 0.20 hour, or 0.20 × 60 = 12 minutes. Across five identical shifts, that error totals 12 × 5 = 60 minutes. These are arithmetic examples, not assumed wage records.

Do not subtract a break just because there is a box

Federal guidance generally counts short rest breaks as hours worked. A meal period is not automatically unpaid just because it is called lunch; relief from work duties matters. The tool requires your confirmed unpaid amount rather than deciding which periods qualify. Applicable state rules and other obligations need separate review.

Three questions before exporting

Is 7:30 the same as 7.30 payroll hours?

No. Seven hours thirty minutes is 7.50 decimal hours. Clock notation counts sixty minutes per hour; a decimal payroll field uses fractions of an hour.

Can the calculator handle an overnight shift?

Yes, when you explicitly select End is next day and no clock change affects elapsed time. Otherwise an end earlier than the start is flagged rather than guessed.

Does the total tell me overtime pay?

No. It totals entered paid time only. Coverage, the employee's workweek, regular-rate calculations and applicable overtime rules require separate review.

Examples and conversion formulas are original arithmetic from stated inputs. No estimated wage, tax rate or software fee is inserted.
