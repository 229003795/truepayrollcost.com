- **Local environment limit, stated plainly:** the bsk browser extension could not attach to a logged-in Chrome instance in this session (a human occupies that browser and each click was revoked with `User activity revoked`). Therefore the candidate set below comes from **web-search-provider ranking plus direct WebFetch of each page body**. It is **not a screenshot/capture of a real SERP from a logged-in Chrome, and US physical location was not verified.** Provider ranking, personalisation and locale can all differ from what a person in the US sees. This is a method bias, not a neutral SERP reconstruction.
- Candidate rule: the first ten **unique** URLs appearing across those three provider result sets that I actually opened and read, in order of appearance. Every URL in the table below was fetched and its body read on 2026-11-27. No URL was added from memory or from a search snippet alone.

## Does the boundary already exist in the top ten?

Honest answer: **partly, and not in the form we need.**

- Four pages (#2, #9, #10 and, outside the ten, the two extra reads) state the payroll-product vs HRIS boundary explicitly and in plain language. So we must **not** claim that nobody has said it.
- Three pages (#3, #8, and #4 on price accuracy) either flatten the two products into one feature table with a winner, or publish "starting prices" with different billing units and no caveat.
- Three pages (#5, #6, #7) are vendor-owned; they state the boundary in the direction that favours their own product and disclaim their competitor data.
- Common weakness across the set: even where the boundary is named, the page then prints a 10/25/50-person side-by-side price table as if the rows were the same purchase, or answers "which is cheaper" anyway. The two sources of the HR-only numbers are largely recycled across pages, and several quote a `$40` Gusto entry price that Gusto's own page does not show.

## Our actual increment (and its limit)

1. Reuse the site's existing batch convention from `/gusto-vs-onpay/`: annualize **only** the vendor with a fully published subscription formula — `(base × 12) + (people × per-person × 12)`, five-worker example, Gusto Simple `$948/year` — and explicitly refuse to annualize the other side into an equal-looking number.
2. Make the scope boundary the conclusion rather than a footnote: one product is payroll + HR in one published subscription; the other is an HRIS that markets payroll inside its platform but publishes no payroll price, arranging it through a demo/quote. **Price level is not judged; only the range/product boundary is.**
3. Give an operating decision rule (payroll + HR together → payroll-first product; payroll already handled → HRIS) without calling a winner or naming any third competitor.
4. Keep the claim modest: we are not first to describe the boundary. We are adding a same-batch, dated, no-price-verdict comparison with a checklist a buyer can take into a quote call.
